# Impossible Color

> Colors that cannot be produced by any single wavelength or physical light mixture -- they exist as mathematical constructs or neural phenomena.

## Overview
Impossible colors are a theoretical concept referring to colors that cannot be produced by real lights or pigments (also termed "imaginary colors"). They exist only as mathematical constructs or as neural phenomena, and they explain why some color spaces include imaginary primaries and why gamut boundaries exist.

## Characteristics
- **Types:**
  - **Imaginary colors:** Points outside the spectral locus in CIE XYZ (negative values).
  - **Chimerical colors:** Perceived through neural adaptation (e.g., "stygian blue").
  - **Opponent colors:** Cannot be perceived simultaneously (reddish-green, bluish-yellow).
- **Why it matters:** Explains why some color spaces include imaginary primaries and why gamut boundaries exist.

## Chromatics API
**Note:** Theoretical concept, not a color model. No API. Useful for the `isImaginary()` method on CIE XYZ / ProPhoto RGB.
